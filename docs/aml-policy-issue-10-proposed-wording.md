# Proposed wording: AML Policy Issue 10 and Risk Assessment Issue 5

For Jack to approve. Nothing here is published: Issue 9 of the policy and
Issue 4 of the risk assessment stay in force until he says "publish".

## Policy, Issue 10

1. **Risk factors (section F, levels of due diligence).** Replace the list of
   automatic enhanced due diligence triggers with: "The following are risk
   factors. Each is assessed by the Nominated Officer or a Deputy, who records
   the outcome as standard, with the reason the risk is mitigated, or as high
   risk. Agents do not make this assessment. A party is not signed off until
   each factor has an outcome: an offshore company or special purpose vehicle
   in the ownership; a limited partnership or unregulated pooled fund; a
   country on FATF's increased monitoring list or BGP's higher-risk list; a
   higher-risk or cash-intensive sector; a name on the US or EU sanctions
   list only."
2. **Always enhanced.** "Enhanced due diligence always applies to a
   politically exposed person, their family and close associates; a party
   established in a FATF call for action country; a trust or similar
   arrangement; and any case the officers assess as high risk."
3. **Newly formed companies.** "A company with no filed accounts is asked how
   the transaction is funded, or for its parent's latest accounts. This is a
   factor to weigh, not a red flag."
4. **Limited partnerships.** "We identify the general partner and any partner
   with more than 25% of the capital, profits or votes, or who otherwise
   controls the partnership. An FCA-authorised manager is recorded and may be
   the officers' reason for standard due diligence."
5. **Pension schemes.** "A SIPP, SSAS or other pension scheme is treated as a
   trust."
6. **Beneficial owners.** Use "more than 25%" throughout.
7. **Discrepancies.** "Where a client tells us the register of people with
   significant control is wrong, an officer decides whether the difference is
   material. A material discrepancy is reported to Companies House."
8. **Sanctions.** "We screen against the UK Sanctions List, which binds us,
   and the US and EU lists. A match on the UK list is reported to OFSI. A
   name on the US or EU list only is a risk factor the officers assess."
9. **Reliance.** "BGP does not rely on another firm's checks. A joint
   agent's documents are checked by BGP; their summary alone does not verify
   identity."
10. **Completion and invoicing.** "BGP cannot prevent a client from
    exchanging or completing. Where checks are outstanding at that point the
    officers are told and decide what to tell the client. BGP does not raise
    its invoice until the checks on the transaction are complete."
11. **Records.** "An excerpt of the register is saved each time it is read. A
    company is asked for its principal place of business where that is not
    its registered office."
12. **Threshold.** A letting is in scope at £10,000 a month or more.

## Risk assessment, Issue 5

1. The risk factors above are scored by the officers case by case; none
   makes a customer high risk by itself except those the law names.
2. FATF increased monitoring: a risk factor to assess (the law requires
   enhanced due diligence for call for action countries only).
3. Cash-intensive operators (restaurants, bars, gaming, car parks): a factor
   to weigh on every transaction.
4. Super-prime value: weighed on every transaction (unchanged from Issue 4).
5. Lenders: recorded where a transaction has one; not a customer of BGP.
